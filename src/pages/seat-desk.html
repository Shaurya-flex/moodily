Seat Desk — a front desk that answers in Hindi and English, and refuses to guess.

A small study library's front desk answers the same questions all day: क्या कल दोपहर सीट खाली है? What is the monthly fee? When do you open? Seat Desk answers them — spoken or typed, Hindi or English — demonstrated with Rasra Library & Study Point as the first business.

Status: a working local demo with genuine live-model evidence. It is not a hosted public app yet — see "Run it yourself" below.

The design rule: the model understands, the code decides

AI assistants fail front desks when they invent facts. Seat Desk splits the job so that cannot happen silently.

🧠 Understanding

Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 turns a sentence — "28 अक्टूबर को दोपहर में कौन सी सीटें खाली हैं?" — into a validated, structured query (date, time slot, intent). It never generates a fact.

🎙 Spoken Hindi

Muse Voice Transcribe 1.0 turns speech into a transcript. The transcript is never used until the speaker confirms or corrects it on screen.

🧮 Facts

Seat availability, hours and fees come from deterministic code over an approved knowledge pack — with the source cited on every policy answer.

🤝 Honest limits

Unknown or consequential requests (refunds, bookings) produce a handoff summary for a human — explicitly not sent. Nothing is ever booked or messaged automatically.

Working today vs planned

✅ Working now

Typed and spoken Hindi/English enquiries · validated live extraction on Muse Spark 1.3 (genuine billed call recorded as evidence in the repository) · deterministic seat availability with overlap handling · cited policy answers · clarification questions · multi-turn follow-ups · explicitly unsent human handoff · a local web desk with per-visitor sessions and layered spending caps · 169 automated tests, including adversarial acceptance where a completed answer with a wrong date, interval or seat count is recorded as a failure.

🔜 Planned, not built

A hosted public demo · connection to a real library's live records (the demo runs on labelled synthetic fixture data) · booking or message delivery (deliberately out of scope today) · a multi-business "approved pack" version for other local businesses — the broader Moodily direction this project points toward.

Architecture in one paragraph

Voice or text enters a local web desk (plain Python, no frameworks). Audio goes to Muse Voice Transcribe over Meta Model API; the transcript must be confirmed by the speaker. Confirmed text goes to Muse Spark 1.3 with a strict JSON schema, minimal reasoning effort, bounded output tokens, zero retries and one outstanding request; the typed, validated extraction (language, intent, date, interval, slot, seat) drives a deterministic availability engine and an approved-policy retrieval layer with citation checks. An independent verifier recomputes every availability answer from the stated query, so a tampered count, interval or seat set is caught. Every response carries its provenance: live or mock understanding, transport, token usage, and a persistent synthetic-data label.

Demonstration data is historical and synthetic (fixture clock 27 October 2026 IST). No real member records exist anywhere in the project, and the real library's membership system is not connected.
